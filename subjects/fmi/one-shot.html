1Accounting in 1 minute

record▶classify▶summarise▶analyse▶communicate
  • = "language of business" → turns money events into info people can decide on
  • why: keep proper records · find profit / loss · show financial position · help decisions · protect assets · stay legal (tax, GST) · inform stakeholders (owners, banks, govt)

2The elements

Assetowned / controlled + gives future benefitcash, stock, machine
Liabilitywhat we OWE outsidersloan, creditors
Equityowner's leftover sharecapital + profits
Incomeearned from businesssales, fees
Expensecost of running / earningrent, salary
Gain / Lossside events up / down equitysell old asset above / below book value

⚠ income · gains → equity ↑  ·  expenses · losses → equity ↓

IMP!!

3Accounting equation

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

→ Equity = Assets − Liabilities  (owner gets what's left)

must balance after every transaction (dual aspect: every txn hits 2 places)

quick check: A ₹8,00,000 · L ₹3,00,000 → E = ₹5,00,000

4Current vs non-current

current (≤ 12 months / 1 cycle)non-current (longer)
assetscash, bank, receivables, inventory, short-term investments, prepaid expensesbuilding, machinery, computers, vehicles, long-term investments, intangibles, right-of-use assets
liabilitiespayables, salaries / interest / tax payable, short-term loanslong-term bank loan, bonds, some lease liabilities

⚠ prepaid expense = ASSET (service still to come) · test = "used / settled within 12 months?"

5Conventions = accepted practice

Consistencysame methods every year (eg same depreciation) → comparable
Prudencecareful: don't overstate assets / income · expect losses, not gains
Full disclosureshow all material info (notes, pending lawsuits)
Materialitymatters if hiding it could change a user's decision · ₹200 stapler → just expense it

concept = assumption about WHAT to record · convention = custom about HOW to present

2What sits on the balance sheet

Non-current assetsPPE: factory, machinery, vehicles, furniture · long-term investments · intangibles (patents, software licences) · right-of-use (lease) assets

3Depreciation

  • spreads PPE cost over its useful life → expense each year it helps earn (= matching)
  • it's an expense, NOT a cash payment → cash left when the asset was bought

SLM (same every year) = (cost − residual) ÷ useful life

₹10,00,000 cost · ₹1,00,000 residual · 5 yrs → 9,00,000 ÷ 5 = ₹1,80,000 / yr

5Cash flow statement — 3 parts

Operatingday-to-day: cash from customers · paid to suppliers, salaries, rent, taxes

2Liquidity — short-term bills

Current ratio = CA ÷ CL

CA ₹6,00,000 ÷ CL ₹3,00,000 = 2 : 1

Quick ratio = (CA − Inventory) ÷ CL

CA 6,00,000 · inventory 2,00,000 · CL 2,00,000 → 4,00,000 ÷ 2,00,000 = 2 : 1 → doesn't depend on selling stock

Cash ratio = (cash + cash equivalents + marketable securities) ÷ CL
current (all CA)− inventory▶ quickonly cash-like▶ cash = strictest

CL = payables, overdraft, short-term loans, accrued expenses, wages, tax payable, current part of LT debt

PYQ ★

3Solvency — long-term survival

Debt-to-equity = Debt ÷ Equity

debt = interest-bearing debt (or total liabilities, as the PPT allows) · equity = share capital + reserves & surplus

debt ₹12 L ÷ equity ₹8 L = 1.5 : 1 → lenders fund more than owners

Interest coverage = EBIT ÷ Interest expense

EBIT ₹10 L ÷ interest ₹2 L = 5 times → higher = safer

⚠ use EBIT (before interest + tax), not PAT

7What happens to the current ratio?

start: CA 6,00,000 · CL 3,00,000 → CR = 2

pay a creditor ₹1 L in cash5 L ÷ 2 L = 2.5 ↑
buy stock ₹1 L on credit7 L ÷ 4 L = 1.75 ↓
take a ₹1 L short-term loan7 L ÷ 4 L = 1.75 ↓
buy a machine ₹1 L in cash5 L ÷ 3 L = 1.67 ↓
collect ₹50,000 from a debtorno change (CA swap)

buy stock for cash → CR same, but quick ratio falls (cash → inventory)

when CR > 1, adding the same amount to both sides pulls it toward 1

4Efficiency — turnover + days

turnover (times)days
inventoryCOGS ÷ avg inventory365 ÷ turnover = holding period
receivablesnet credit sales ÷ avg receivables365 ÷ turnover = collection period
payablescredit purchases ÷ avg payables365 ÷ turnover = payment period
average = (opening + closing) ÷ 2

eg inventory: opening 1,00,000 · closing 1,40,000 · COGS 6,00,000

avg inventory = (1,00,000 + 1,40,000) ÷ 2 = 1,20,000
turnover      = 6,00,000 ÷ 1,20,000      = 5 times
holding days  = 365 ÷ 5                  = 73 days
  • turnover ↑ = stock / debtors move faster · days ↓ = better (for payables, more days = longer supplier credit)
  • receivables use CREDIT sales · payables use CREDIT purchases (or the basis the question gives)

1Margin vs return

  • margin = profit ÷ revenue · return = profit ÷ capital / assets / equity
  • good liquidity can still mean poor profit · high sales can have thin margins · high profit can still be a poor return on capital · high ROE may just be heavy debt
IMP!!

2Profit margins

Revenue          20,00,000
− COGS           12,00,000
= Gross profit    8,00,000  → GPM = 8 ÷ 20 = 40%
− operating exp.
= Operating profit (EBIT)  → OPM = EBIT ÷ revenue
− interest − tax
= PAT                      → NPM = PAT ÷ revenue
GPMgross profit ÷ revenue × 100after COGS only
OPMoperating profit ÷ revenue × 1003 L ÷ 15 L = 20%
NPMPAT ÷ revenue × 1001.5 L ÷ 10 L = 15%

always GPM ≥ OPM ≥ NPM (each step takes more costs out)

★★★

3ROCE · ROA · ROE

profit÷ baseeg
ROCEEBITcapital employed12 L ÷ 60 L = 20%
ROAPATavg total assets5 L ÷ 50 L = 10%
ROEPATavg shareholders' equity4 L ÷ 20 L = 20%
capital employed = total assets − current liabilities = equity + long-term debt

avg = (opening + closing) ÷ 2 · equity = share capital + retained earnings

⚠ ROCE → EBIT (it's funded by debt + equity) · ROA, ROE → PAT

4ROI

ROI = (gain − cost) ÷ cost × 100

cost ₹50,000 · gain ₹65,000 → 15,000 ÷ 50,000 = 30%

broad measure for one investment · no single official accounting formula

★★

5DuPont — why ROE is what it is

ROE = NPM × asset turnover × equity multiplier

= (PAT ÷ revenue) × (revenue ÷ assets) × (assets ÷ equity)

PAT 1 L · revenue 10 L · assets 5 L · equity 2.5 L

10% × 2× × 2× = 40% (= 1 L ÷ 2.5 L ✓)

⚠ big multiplier = lots of debt → high ROE can mean high risk

6Capital employed, worked

total assets 50 L · current liabilities 10 L → CE = 40 L

EBIT 6 L → ROCE = 6 ÷ 40 = 15% · compare with interest rate on loans: ROCE > rate → borrowing pays off

7EPS & P/E

EPS = (PAT − preference dividend) ÷ no. of ordinary shares

PAT 8,00,000 − pref 80,000 = 7,20,000 ÷ 1,20,000 shares = ₹6

P/E = market price per share ÷ EPS

₹240 ÷ ₹12 = 20 times → investors pay ₹20 for every ₹1 of earnings

⚠ high P/E = growth hopes OR overpriced → not automatically good / bad

★★★

8Dividends

DPStotal ordinary dividends ÷ ordinary shares3 L ÷ 1 L = ₹3
payout ratioDPS ÷ EPS × 1006 ÷ 24 = 25%
dividend yieldDPS ÷ market price × 1008 ÷ 200 = 4%

payout = share of PROFIT paid out · yield = income vs the PRICE u pay

⚠ don't flip them (EPS ÷ DPS ✗) · slide titled "Dividend Per Share (EPS)" = typo, it's DPS

9Market cap & enterprise value

market cap = share price × shares outstanding

₹300 × 24 lakh = ₹72 crore · reverse: price = market cap ÷ shares → 72 Cr ÷ 24 L = ₹300

EV = market cap + total debt − cash

500 Cr + 150 Cr − 50 Cr = ₹600 crore → used in valuation, M&A

market capvalue of the equity only
EVwhole business: equity + debt − cash (buyer takes on debt, gets cash)

market cap = a value, not technically a ratio
